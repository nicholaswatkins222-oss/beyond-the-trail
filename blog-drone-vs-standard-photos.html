Drone vs. Standard Listing Photos: What Reno Buyers Actually Click On

Aerial photography boosts listing clicks by 68%. Here's when drone shots make the biggest difference — and when standard ground photos are enough.

March 2026 Josh Young · Beyond the Trail Media 4 min read

Here's something that consistently surprises realtors: on Zillow and Realtor.com, listings with drone photography receive 68% more clicks than those with ground photos only. That's not a marginal improvement — it's a fundamentally different level of buyer attention.

But drone photography isn't the right call for every listing. Knowing when aerial shots make the biggest difference — and when they're overkill — is how you get the most out of your marketing budget.

What Buyers Are Actually Looking For in Listing Photos

When a buyer opens a listing on Zillow, they're making a gut decision in about 20 seconds: does this feel like a place I want to live? That decision is made almost entirely on visual information.

Ground photos answer: What does the inside look like? Is the kitchen nice? Is it well-maintained?

Drone photos answer: Where is this property? How big is the lot? What's the neighborhood like? Is there a view? How close are the mountains / the freeway / the amenities?

For buyers relocating to Reno from out of state — a large portion of the Northern Nevada market — those drone questions are often the deciding factor before they book a flight to visit in person.

When Drone Photos Make the Biggest Difference

1. Large lots, acreage, or rural properties

If your listing has more than a quarter acre, drone shots are almost always worth it. Ground photos simply can't convey lot size or how the property sits on the land. Buyers want to see the full picture — drone shots give them that.

2. Properties with views

Reno has spectacular views — the Sierra Nevada to the west, the Virginia Range to the east, and the valley in between. If your listing has a view, drone photography captures what buyers are actually paying for. A ground photo of a window with a view doesn't do it justice.

3. New construction or spec homes

Aerial shots show how a new build sits in its lot and neighborhood — critical context for buyers who can't physically visit before making an offer. Drone photos also show proximity to parks, schools, and amenities in a way that no map screenshot can.

4. Vacant land listings

For raw land or vacant lots, drone photography isn't optional — it's the primary product. A ground shot of dirt and scrub brush tells buyers almost nothing. Aerial shots show topography, access roads, neighboring development, and potential.

5. Luxury or premium listings

At the $600K+ price point in Reno, buyers expect a full visual package. Drone photos are part of the premium presentation that justifies the listing price and attracts serious buyers willing to pay it.

💡 A general rule: if the property has anything visually interesting above eye level — a view, a large lot, a rooftop deck, mountain proximity — drone shots will outperform ground-only listings.

When Standard Ground Photos Are Enough

Drone photography isn't always necessary. Here's when standard MLS photos do the job:

Standard photos work well for:

  • Small urban lots with little outdoor space
  • Condos and townhomes in dense developments
  • Properties where the interior is the primary selling point
  • Listings where the neighborhood context is neutral

Add drone shots when you have:

  • Lots larger than 0.25 acres
  • Mountain, valley, or water views
  • Outdoor amenities (pool, large yard, RV parking)
  • Rural or semi-rural location
  • Luxury price point ($500K+)

Drone Photography in the Reno Context

Northern Nevada has some unique advantages for drone photography. The Reno area's clear skies, dramatic terrain changes, and the contrast between the urban core and the surrounding high desert make for compelling aerial shots.

Spanish Springs, South Meadows, Damonte Ranch, and the foothills west of Reno all have properties where drone shots showcase the landscape in a way that ground photos simply cannot. If you're listing in these areas, aerial photography is almost always the right call.
